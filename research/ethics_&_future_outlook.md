# Ethics and Future Outlook of REA

## Ethics

REA(Reverse Engineer Anything) is a tool that lets an AI assistant study how an app or program works, even when you can’t see the code inside the app or program. That can be helpful, but it can also be misused. The creators say it should only be used legally and with permission, and the user is responsible for following the law (Antony, 2026). Antony argues this warning should come first when talking about REA, not after the exciting demos. Having an AI do the work doesn’t make it okay to break a license, a contract, or someone else’s security rules. Horsey (2026) points to copying a competitor’s product and getting around copy protection as the main ways it could be misused.

Antony also points out two risks that are easy to miss:

- Local doesn’t mean private. REA does its analysis on your own computer, but the AI assistant still receives the results, and the company behind the AI has its own rules about what it keeps. Private information could still leave your computer.

- The program being studied may be ran. REA can sometimes run the app it’s examining, which could affect your own machine. Which would not be ideal if analysing malware.

Antony also says the AI’s findings are clues to investigate, not finished answers to copy. A person still needs to decide whether it’s okay to build something new based on what was learned.

## Future Outlook

Antony expects the most lasting use to be modest. An AI gathers evidence about software you’re allowed to examine, shows how it reached its conclusions, and admits what it doesn’t know, while a human makes the final call. Hard cases don’t become easy just because an AI explains the results. For software makers, Horsey (2026) suggests keeping important parts of an app on their own servers, protecting user data, and focusing on a great user experience. The idea is that if an app’s inner workings are easier to study, its value has to come from things that can’t be copied from the outside.
